# GymBuddy exercise library — research

8 Nov 2026 · researched for a boy (7–12) in competitive artistic gymnastics, training at home on a **BenchK 733B**:
wall bars (Swedish ladder, 240 cm, beech rungs), a removable pull-up bar, a dip bar and a workout bench that hook onto the rungs, plus floor space.

**Have the coach look at this list** before exercises are added to his plan, and check the Dutch names in the app.
Every exercise below is in the app's Moves tab with an animated drawing, cues, safety notes and links to these sources.

## How this was researched

Three passes of web research (federations and gymnastics coaches; wall-bar and equipment guides; youth strength
and flexibility guidance from medical bodies). About 45 pages on 35 different sites were used. Sources marked
*search only* could not be opened (PDF or access blocked); only their search summary was used, so they back up a
point rather than carry it.

**Gaps, honestly:** there is no official BenchK exercise guide (BenchK-branded "blog" pages that turn up in search are
unofficial copies and were left out). The Dutch federation (KNGU) has no home conditioning programme online. No
physio-written split protocol for children was found. Exercises marked *common practice* are standard gymnastics
conditioning that no opened source described in detail.

## Safety for children at home

From the medical and federation sources above:

- **How often:** 2–3 short home sessions a week on non-consecutive days, at least one full rest day, and 8–12 hours of sleep. Club training counts too: if he already trains many hours, keep home work short. [bjsm-youth, nhs, boston-childrens, gymnz]
- **How much:** start with 1–2 sets at easy-to-moderate effort; later 2–4 sets of 6–12 reps, holds of 10–30 s. Move up a step after 3 clean sets of 8. Technique first. [bjsm-youth, rbwf-routine, ghq-principles]
- **Weights:** bodyweight work comes first for gymnasts, but light free weights are fine for children with supervision: a weight he can lift about 15 times with clean form, no maximal lifts, technique checked by an adult. Weight training does not stunt growth. [aap-resistance, bjsm-youth, nsca-ltad, sporza, ghq-home]
- **Supervision and setup:** an adult nearby, a mat under the wall bars, climb only to a safe height. Check that the dip bar and bench hooks sit fully on a rung every time. Learn new skills at the club first. [schn, ghq-home, veiligheid, szeged]
- **Wall-bar mounting:** solid masonry of at least 12 cm or wooden studs, never plasterboard alone; ceiling at least 250 cm; re-tighten the fixings in the first month. [fitshop-benchk, peak-stall]
- **Flexibility:** stretch to mild discomfort, never pain; no oversplits (past a flat split); no forced back bends; walkovers only at the club. Extra care in growth spurts, when flexibility drops. If he is very bendy (hypermobile), build strength rather than more stretch. [ghq-flexibility, dance-teacher, aap-gymnastics]
- **Wrists:** build load gradually. Thumb-side wrist pain that is worse when weight-bearing, or swelling the next day: stop handstands and supports and see a doctor. [childrens-mercy, aap-gymnastics]
- **Lower back:** low-back pain that lasts more than two weeks and gets worse with arching needs a doctor's check (stress fracture risk). In bridges, open the shoulders instead of bending more in the lower back. [aap-gymnastics, boston-childrens, usag-tops]
- **Knees:** good landing technique and hamstring strength protect the knees (ACL). [aap-gymnastics, tilley-legs]
- **Long term:** strength is built over years; watch for burnout and let him have a say. [fig-mag, nsca-ltad, aap-gymnastics]

### Left out on purpose
Oversplits (past flat), walkovers or limbers at home, bridge walk-down on the wall bars, human flag, kettlebell swings
(technique to learn with a coach), heavy or maximal lifts, and weighted pull-ups for children (adults only): the
sources advise against these at home or for this age, or reserve them for the club.
